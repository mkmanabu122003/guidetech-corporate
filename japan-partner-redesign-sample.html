The gap is rarely translation alone.

Most early Japan attempts fail because no one owns the local operating details between product, sales, suppliers, support, and HQ reporting.

01 Japanese inquiries arrive, but no one can answer naturally.

Response speed, tone, escalation, and next-step clarity matter before the first customer meeting.

02 The product is translated, but still feels foreign.

UI length, honorific tone, names, dates, invoices, and help flows need product-context decisions.

03 Supplier search becomes a list of names, not a decision.

Japanese outreach, fit checks, pricing signals, availability, and risk notes need local handling.

04 HQ cannot tell whether Japan has traction or just noise.

Weekly reporting should separate buyer evidence, operational blockers, and recommended actions.
