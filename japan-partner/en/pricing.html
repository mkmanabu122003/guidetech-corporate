What actually drives the price

These are the variables we ask about on the readiness call. Knowing your answers in advance makes the quote faster and tighter.

Volume and surface

How many words, screens, SKUs or suppliers are in scope, and whether the work is a one-time piece or a recurring flow.

Depth of review

Whether we work from supplied text alone, or need product context, screenshots, interface checks and terminology decisions.

Research effort

How many candidate suppliers need finding, contacting in Japanese and screening before a shortlist is credible.

Response expectations

How quickly Japanese-language replies to customers or suppliers need to go out, and on which days.

Operational role

Whether we only advise, or also hold the relationship, coordinate meetings and handle the follow-through.

Duration

A single scope, a fixed pilot period, or an ongoing monthly desk. Longer commitments are scoped differently from one-off work.

Pricing FAQ

The commercial questions overseas teams ask first.

Why are there no published rates?

Because the same request varies by an order of magnitude depending on volume, depth of review and how much of the relationship we hold. A published rate would either be meaningless or wrong for you. The readiness call is free, and the quote that follows is fixed for the scope it describes.

What currency do you invoice in?

Tell us what your finance team needs during scoping and we will confirm what we can accommodate before you commit to anything.

Is there a minimum commitment?

The readiness review carries no commitment. Project scopes are agreed as a single defined deliverable. For the monthly desk, the starting term is agreed at scoping so both sides can end it cleanly if the fit turns out to be wrong.

Can we start with something small?

Yes, and we prefer it. A narrow first scope — one supplier shortlist, one localization review — tells both sides more than a long proposal does.
